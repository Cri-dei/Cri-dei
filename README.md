# Hi, I'm Cristina Deidda 👋

I am a **researcher in environmental statistics and climate data analysis**, specializing in **extreme and compound events, statistical modelling, and climate change attribution**.

My research focuses on developing and applying **statistical methods to understand complex climate extremes**, moving beyond individual events to investigate **multivariate, co-occurring, and consecutive extremes** and how their probability and impacts change under different climate conditions.

I am currently a **Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral Fellow at Vrije Universiteit Brussel (VUB)**, working at the interface of statistics, climate modelling, hydrology, and climate-impact assessment.

### 🔬 Research focus

* **Compound, multivariate & consecutive extreme events**
* Development of **statistical methods for climate extremes**
* **Detection & attribution** of climate change
* Extreme value theory & multivariate statistics
* Copulas & dependence modelling
* Climate model & large-ensemble analysis
* Climate impacts & risk assessment
* Hydrological extremes

A central goal of my research is to develop statistical approaches that move beyond the traditional analysis of single hazards and isolated events. My work investigates **dependence between extremes, co-occurring hazards, and temporal clustering**, providing methods to quantify how these complex events change in a warming climate.

### 📚 Selected research

**Attribution of compound and consecutive extremes**
Development of statistical frameworks to quantify how climate change modifies the probability of **co-occurring and repeated extreme events**, integrating extreme-value statistics, multivariate dependence modelling, climate models, and temporal attribution approaches.

**Climate extremes and European transport infrastructure**
Large-scale climate-impact assessment using **ISIMIP climate-impact data and climate model projections** to investigate the exposure of the Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T) to **heatwaves, droughts, river floods, and wildfires**. The analysis combines extreme-event indicators, multiple climate scenarios, and spatial infrastructure data to assess how climate risks evolve under future warming and how climate mitigation can reduce exposure.
→ *Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences* (2026)

**Asymmetric dependence in hydrological extremes**
Development of **asymmetric tail Kendall's τ**, a statistical measure designed to identify directional dependence between extreme events and investigate asymmetric dependence structures.
→ *Water Resources Research* (2023)
DOI: 10.1029/2023WR034512

**Causes of dependence between extreme floods**
Statistical investigation of the hydrological and climatological mechanisms underlying **spatial dependence between extreme river floods**.
→ *Environmental Research Letters* (2021)
DOI: 10.1088/1748-9326/ac07d5

**Satellite altimetry for river monitoring**
Statistical evaluation of **Copernicus satellite observations** for water-level monitoring in Italian rivers, comparing satellite-derived and in-situ observations.
→ *Remote Sensing* (2021)
DOI: 10.3390/rs13214456
