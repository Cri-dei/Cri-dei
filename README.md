# Hi, I'm Cristina Deidda 👋

I am a **researcher in statistics and climate science** specializing in **climate extremes, compound events, and climate change attribution**.

My research focuses on developing and applying **new statistical methods to understand complex climate extremes**, moving beyond the analysis of individual events to investigate **multivariate, co-occurring, and consecutive extremes** and how their probability and impacts are changing in a warming climate.

I am currently a **Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral Fellow at Vrije Universiteit Brussel (VUB)**, working at the interface of climate science, statistics, hydrology, and climate-impact assessment.

### 🔬 Research focus

* **Compound and consecutive climate extremes**
* **Detection & Multivariate and compound attribution of climate change**
* Development of **new statistical methods for climate extremes**
* Extreme value theory & multivariate statistics
* Copulas & extremal dependence
* Climate impacts & risk assessment
* Hydrological extremes

A central goal of my research is to extend event-attribution and extreme-value approaches beyond single hazards and isolated events, developing frameworks that account for **dependence between hazards and the temporal clustering of extremes**.

### 🌍 ECMWF / Copernicus Climate Change Service

I contribute to the **ECMWF Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S)**, supporting the **quality assessment and quality control of C3S climate datasets**.

### 📚 Selected research

**Attribution of compound and consecutive extremes**
Development of statistical frameworks to quantify how climate change modifies the probability of **co-occurring and repeated extreme events**, combining multivariate dependence modelling with temporal attribution approaches. Collaboration with World Weather Attribution.

**Climate impacts on European transport infrastructure**
Large-scale climate-impact assessment using ISIMIP climate-impact data and climate model projections to investigate the exposure of the Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T) to heatwaves, droughts, river floods, and wildfires. The analysis combines extreme-event indicators, multiple climate scenarios, and spatial infrastructure data to assess how climate risks evolve under future warming and how climate mitigation can reduce exposure. Collaboration with DG-Move, European Commission.
→ *Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences* (2026)

**Asymmetric dependence in hydrological extremes**
Development of **asymmetric tail Kendall's τ**, a new statistical measure for identifying directional dependence between extreme events.
→ *Water Resources Research* (2023)
DOI: 10.1029/2023WR034512

**Causes of dependence between extreme floods**
Investigation of the hydrological and climatological mechanisms underlying spatial dependence between extreme river floods.
→ *Environmental Research Letters* (2021)
DOI: 10.1088/1748-9326/ac07d5

**Satellite altimetry for river monitoring**
Evaluation of Copernicus satellite altimetry for water-level monitoring in Italian rivers.
→ *Remote Sensing* (2021)
DOI: 10.3390/rs13214456
